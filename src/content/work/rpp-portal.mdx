## Problem

Undergraduate research positions at a university are matched almost entirely
through informal channels — a professor mentions an opening in a lecture, or a
student emails around hoping to catch one. Faculty have no clean way to advertise
what they need, and students have no way to see what exists or to present the
coursework that would qualify them.

The portal gives both sides a structured surface: faculty post positions with
concrete requirements, students maintain a profile of majors, courses taken,
programming languages, and interests, and applications carry references.

## Data

The interesting part is the relational model, because the matching problem is
really a modelling problem. Users are polymorphic — `Student` and `Faculty` both
inherit a shared `User` — and the qualification surface is normalised across
`Major`, `Course`, `TakenCourse`, `ProgrammingLang`, and `TopicOfInterest`.
`Position`, `Application`, and `Reference` carry the workflow itself.

- **Store:** SQLAlchemy models, Flask-Migrate migrations, SQLite locally and PostgreSQL on AWS RDS in production
- **Scale:** a course project, so the data is seeded rather than organic — no real user volume behind it

## Approach

Flask with blueprint separation — `auth`, `student`, `faculty`, `main`, `errors` —
so the two user roles stay isolated rather than tangled behind conditionals.

Authentication goes through **Auth0 via OAuth**: the app redirects to Auth0,
exchanges the token, and keys the local user record on the returned `auth0_sub`
rather than storing passwords at all. `itsdangerous` handles signed tokens and
Flask-Mail covers verification flows. Sessions ride on Flask-Login.

Deployment is containerised with Docker and runs on AWS EC2 backed by RDS
PostgreSQL, with HTTPS terminated at the instance and environment-based config
separating local from production.

Process was Agile across four people over six weeks, with the team liaison role
carrying communication with the course staff. 174 commits, of which I authored
60 — the largest share on the team.
